# Mechanics

Rules as specified by the owner. **[GEN]** = generated by Claude as a tunable first pass, not
owner-authored truth. **[OPEN]** = unresolved, see [OPEN-QUESTIONS.md](OPEN-QUESTIONS.md).

---

## 1. Time

| Unit | Value |
|---|---|
| Tick (day phase) | 1 real second at 1x |
| Day | 4 ticks — `1 Night`, `2 Morning`, `3 Noon`, `4 Evening` |
| Month ("turn") | 30 days = **120 ticks** = 120s at 1x |
| Year | 12 months = 1440 ticks |
| Battle turn | 1 in-game hour → **6 battle turns per tick** |

- Speeds: **Pause**, `1x`, `2x`, `5x`, `10x`. There is **no End Turn**.
- The simulation **pauses when the player is not in the game** (tab hidden, app backgrounded,
  device locked). No offline progress.
- Continuous: movement, construction, recruitment, income, AI decisions.
- Campaign starts **January 1350**.

## 2. Victory

Conquer every other faction. All factions permanently hostile; no diplomacy in v1.

## 3. Map & territory

- [`data/maps/europe-1350.json`](../data/maps/europe-1350.json) — 70 × 35 square tiles,
  58 cities, 42 resource nodes. Validated.
- A tile glyph `C`/`G`/`S`/`I`/`T` marks a feature; the tile's **underlying terrain** is in the
  feature record's own `terrain` field.
- The map view is **zoomable and draggable**.

### Ownership
- Each faction starts with **one Village-tier city plus its 4 orthogonal neighbour tiles**,
  and **250 gold**. Water neighbours are skipped — territory is a land concept until naval
  exists, and otherwise coastal capitals would open owning open sea. **[GEN]**
- **Territory is claimed by army presence**: a tile becomes yours when one of your armies
  enters it. There is no passive border growth.
- The other **45 cities** belong to a neutral **Independents** faction and are garrisoned.

### Armies
- **One army per tile.** No stacking. Two friendly armies meeting on a tile **merge**.
- Up to **20 units** per army. A unit is a body of soldiers; size varies by type.
- Army strategic speed = **its slowest unit**.
- Movement is tile-by-tile, paid down across the ticks of the month. Armies move
  **orthogonally**; diagonals would let an army cross √2 tiles for the price of one. **[GEN]**
- A march is **banked in integer march points**: one tile of open ground costs
  `120 × 100` points and an army banks `speed × 100` a tick, so the stated speed in tiles per
  month is exactly what a player gets, at any game speed.
- A march order **appends to the route already given**, routing the new leg from the end of the
  last one, so a journey can be laid out in several clicks. **Halt** cancels the whole route,
  queued legs and all — it is the only way to throw a route away.

### Garrison, defenders and the field

A settlement holds three separate things, and they behave quite differently.

| | Where it comes from | Costs | Can it leave? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Defenders** | Derived from tier and buildings | Nothing | Never |
| **Garrison** | Units the faction recruited | Gold up front, then upkeep | Mustered into an army |
| **Fleet** | Ships the faction built | Gold and wood, then upkeep | Not until naval |

**Defenders** are the reason no settlement can be walked into. Composition is owner-authored:

| Tier | Defenders |
|---|---|
| Village | 1 Light Infantry |
| Town | 1 Light Infantry, 1 Sword & Shield |
| City | 1 Light Infantry, 1 Sword & Shield, 1 Archer |
| Capitol | 1 Light Infantry, 1 Spear, 1 Sword & Shield, 1 Archer, 1 Light Cavalry |

Plus **one more for each of Barracks, Archery Range, Stables and Town Hall** — the unit that
building trains, and militia for the Town Hall. **[GEN]** mapping; the owner named the
buildings, not the units. A fully built Capitol therefore fields **nine** free defenders.

Because defenders are derived and never stored, a settlement that gains a tier or finishes a
Barracks is stronger the moment the work completes, and no save can disagree with the rules.

### What stops a march

Borders do not. Open ground is walkable whoever owns it, and entering a tile **claims it**.
An army is stopped by:

- **water** — until transports exist;
- **a hostile army**, which will start a battle once combat exists;
- **a hostile settlement**, which is always defended, so it can never simply be walked into;
- **a friendly army**, unless the march ends there, in which case the two merge.

Every faction opens the campaign with **one Light Infantry** in its capital's garrison. The
Independents get none — 47 free units would arm the neutrals better than anyone.

## 4. Terrain

Modifier scale, as restated by the owner — note it is **not symmetric**:

| Symbol | Effect | Multiplier |
|---|---|---|
| `++` | +100% | ×2 |
| `+` | +50% | ×1.5 |
| base | — | ×1 |
| `-` | **−40%** | ×0.6 |
| `--` | −80% | ×0.2 |

**Defender's advantage of X%** = defender deals **+X%** damage, attacker deals **−X%**.

| Terrain | Def. adv. | Move penalty | Farms | Mines | Sawmill | Roads |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Water `~` | — | — | unbuildable | unbuildable | unbuildable | ships only |
| Plains `.` | 10% | — | `++` | `--` | base | path, paved, stone |
| Forest `*` | 20% | 25% | base | `--` | `++` | path, paved |
| Steppe `:` | 10% | — | `+` | `-` | base | path, paved, stone |
| Desert `_` | 20% | 50% | `--` | `--` | `--` | **[OPEN]** tiers |
| Tundra `%` | 10% | 25% | `-` | `+` | `++` | **[OPEN]** tiers |
| Mountain `^` | 30% | 50% | `--` | `++` | `+` | **[OPEN]** tiers |

**City tile:** underlying terrain's defender advantage **+10%**, plus the settlement's
fortification bonus (see [CONTENT.md](CONTENT.md) §2).

### Resource nodes

42 nodes on the map: 9 gold, 9 silver, 14 iron, 10 stone. Yields are in §5 — a node pays a
token amount for merely being owned, and a great deal more once a mine is built on it.

## 5. Economy

Resources: **gold, wood, iron, stone**. There is no food resource — **farms produce gold**.

Income and upkeep accrue **per tick** at 1/120th of the monthly rate, in fixed-point integers
so nothing is lost to rounding and totals stay deterministic. Costs are paid **up front**;
production then runs for its stated number of months.

### Population

Every city has a population. It starts at **1,000** and compounds **monthly**.

Monthly growth rate is the sum of:

| Source | Rate |
|---|---|
| Base | +0.1% |
| Treasury | see the wealth table below |
| City level | +0.1% per level (Village 1 → Capitol 4) |
| Housing level | +0.1% per level |
| Hall building | +0.1% each — Town Hall, City Hall, Palace |

A starting city — Village, Wooden Houses, 250 gold, no hall — grows at
`0.1 + 0 + 0.1 + 0.1 = 0.3%` per month.

**Every 100 people yields 1 gold per month.** A 1,000-population village is worth 10 gold/month.

#### Wealth bonus — treasury-wide, diminishing, and symmetric

The bonus applies to **every city at once** and is calculated from the **faction treasury**,
with diminishing returns so a late-game fortune does not run away with the game.

**Debt hurts exactly as much as wealth helps.** The same bands run in reverse, so a realm
10,000 gold in the red loses 1% of its people a month.

| Treasury | Growth |
|---|---|
| 1,000,000 gold | +3.0% |
| 100,000 gold | +2.0% |
| 10,000 gold | +1.0% |
| −10,000 gold | −1.0% |
| −100,000 gold | −2.0% |
| −1,000,000 gold | −3.0% |

A settlement never falls below **100 people** however deep the debt. **[GEN]**

Implemented as integer tenths of a percent, capped at 30 (+3%):

| Band | Step |
|---|---|
| below 10,000 | +0.1% per 1,000 gold |
| 10,000 – 99,999 | 10 tenths, plus 0.1% per 10,000 above 10,000 |
| 100,000 – 999,999 | 20 tenths, plus 0.1% per 100,000 above 100,000 |
| 1,000,000+ | 30 tenths (capped) |

The owner's three anchor values are exact. Band edges step from 18 → 20 and 28 → 30 rather
than passing through 19 and 29, because the stated anchors and the stated per-band steps do
not divide evenly. **[GEN]** — flagged in case the anchors matter more than the smoothness.

Whether population is capped, whether it can fall, what housing tier a Capitol has, and
whether recruiting consumes population are still **[OPEN]**.

### Tile improvements

**Farms, mines and sawmills are tile improvements, not city buildings.**

- A tile carries **exactly one** improvement — farm, mine, or sawmill. Choosing is permanent
  in the sense that the tile cannot host the other two. *(Whether it can be demolished and
  replaced is **[OPEN]**.)*
- An improvement is **built at level 1** and can be **upgraded three more times**, to a
  maximum of **level 4**.
- The **realm's highest settlement tier caps the level** of every improvement it owns. A
  faction with only Villages is stuck at level 1 everywhere; a Capitol unlocks level 4 across
  the whole realm. This is the main reason to push a settlement up the tiers.
- A **city tile is also a tile**: a settlement carries an improvement of its own alongside its
  city buildings, under the same rules.

**Every held land tile pays a base yield each month**, improved or not — territory pays for
itself from the month it is taken. The yield depends on the terrain, and improvements add on
top of it.

| Terrain | Base yield per month |
|---|---|
| Plains `.` | 10 gold, 1 wood |
| Forest `*` | 5 gold, 1 wood |
| Steppe `:` | 5 gold |
| Tundra `%` | 2 gold, 1 wood |
| Desert `_` | 2 gold |
| Mountain `^` | 1 iron, 1 stone |
| Water `~` | nothing |

These numbers are **not** run through the terrain modifiers in §4. They already *are* the
terrain's contribution; scaling them again would count the terrain twice. Only the
improvement's own yield is scaled.

Note that **mountain pays no gold at all**. A realm boxed in by mountains starts rich in metal
and starved of coin, which is why a capital's surrounding terrain is a balance decision and not
just scenery.

| Improvement | Produces | L1 | L2 | L3 | L4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Farm | gold | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 |
| Sawmill | wood | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Mine | see the mine table below | | | | |

**Wood is deliberately scarce.** A sawmill yields single digits, and beyond the trickle a
plains, forest or tundra tile gives for free, nothing else in the game produces wood, so the
100 wood a Town upgrade demands is a real campaign of forestry. Forest doubles the sawmill,
tundra doubles it, desert floors it to nothing.

Terrain then scales the result using the modifiers in §4, and the result is **floored** to stay
integer. A level-1 farm on plains yields 20 gold/month; a level-4 farm on plains yields 80; the
same farm in the desert yields 2.

Seasonal harvest modifiers apply to farm output only.

### Mines and resource nodes

A resource node pays a token amount for merely being **owned**. Building and upgrading a
**mine** on it is where the real money is — a fully upgraded gold mine is worth ten times the
bare tile.

Monthly yield by mine level, **before** the terrain modifier:

| Node | Owned, no mine | Mine 1 | Mine 2 | Mine 3 | Mine 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gold `G` | 20 gold | 50 | 100 | 200 | 400 **[GEN]** |
| Silver `S` | 10 gold | 25 | 50 | 100 | 200 **[GEN]** |
| Iron `I` | 1 iron | 3 | 5 | 7 | 9 **[GEN]** |
| Stone `T` | 1 stone | 3 | 5 | 7 | 9 **[GEN]** |

Silver feeds the **gold** stockpile — it is a gold mine by another name.

A mine on an **ordinary tile** — no node — produces **both** metals:

| Mine level | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Iron *and* stone | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 **[GEN]** |

This makes the 42 nodes worth fighting for without making ordinary territory worthless.

**Terrain modifiers apply to mines and sawmills exactly as they do to farms.** A level-3 gold
mine in the mountains (`Mines++`) pays 400/month; the same mine on plains (`Mines--`) pays 40.

Level 4 values are **[GEN]**: the owner specified four improvement levels but only three mine
levels, so level 4 continues each series' own pattern — gold and silver double, iron and stone
add 2, an ordinary mine adds 1.

### Construction

**Three independent queues per settlement** — buildings, recruitment and ships. Only the head
of each advances, but the three advance in parallel: a city can raise walls, train spearmen and
lay a keel in the same month. If a unit's prerequisite buildings already stand, it can be
trained while other construction continues.

Ships need the naval line: Dock gives Transport and Light Ship, Port adds Heavy Ship, Shipyard
adds Flagship.

### Upkeep and debt

Every unit and ship costs gold per month, netted off income so the treasury figure is what a
faction actually banks.

**Gold may go negative.** A realm can run into debt, and pays for it twice:

- **Population shrinks**, through the symmetric wealth band above.
- **Troops desert.** Every month a faction's treasury is in the red, each unit in every
  garrison has a **10% chance** of walking away. A notification says where and how many.

Desertion is drawn from the seeded RNG, over a fixed iteration order, so it stays reproducible
from a save. Ships are **not** subject to desertion — the owner specified armies. **[OPEN]**
whether a fleet should suffer the same.

| Work | Duration |
|---|---|
| Building, level 1 | 12 months |
| Each further building level | +6 months (18, 24, 30) |
| Village → Town | 24 months |
| Town → City | 36 months |
| City → Capitol | 48 months |

Costs are paid up front when the item is queued.

### Seasons

| Season | Months | Movement | Farm income | Other |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Winter | Dec–Feb | −40% | −60% | **+10% defender's advantage** |
| Spring | Mar–May | — | — | — |
| Summer | Jun–Aug | — | — | — |
| Autumn | Sep–Nov | — | +25% (harvest) | — |

Movement and farm numbers approved by the owner; winter defender bonus is owner-specified.

## 6. Combat

### Trigger
An army moving onto a tile held by a hostile army, or onto a hostile city tile, starts a battle.

### Framing
- Resolved as **battle turns of 1 in-game hour, 6 per tick**.
- v1 is **auto-resolve only**, but simulated turn by turn so the player can **open the battle
  and watch it**.
- A battle involving the **player pauses the campaign** and prompts. In v1 the only option is
  *Auto Resolve*. This prompt is the seam where the Phase B tactical battle map plugs in.
- AI-vs-AI battles resolve without pausing.

### Auto-resolve algorithm — owner-specified
1. The two armies start **10 tiles apart**.
2. Turn order alternates by speed: **attacker's fastest unit, defender's fastest, attacker's
   next-fastest, defender's next-fastest**, and so on.
3. On activation a unit takes **one action** — *Move* toward the enemy (up to its battle
   speed in tiles), or *Attack* a **random enemy unit** within range.
4. Ranged units may fire once an enemy is inside their range — so a unit that closes into an
   archer's range can be shot before it arrives.
5. Damage is applied to the target's soldier pool; dead soldiers are **deducted from the
   unit's soldier count**.

### End conditions
- **Any time:** one army is destroyed.
- **After turn 10:** one army has **more than 3× the other's unit count** → the smaller routs.
- **Hard cap: 48 battle turns.** Outcome at the cap is **[OPEN]**.

### Damage formula — **[GEN]**, owner to approve
```
raw       = attacker.soldiers × attacker.damagePerSoldier
modified  = raw × chargeMod × matchupMod × (1 − rangedResist) × (1 − defenderAdvantage)
casualties = floor(modified / target.hpPerSoldier)
```
- `chargeMod` — applied **once**, on the turn a unit closes into melee contact:
  shock infantry `+50 flat per soldier`, light cavalry `×2`, heavy cavalry `×3`.
- `matchupMod` — spear infantry `×2` vs cavalry.
- `rangedResist` — `0.20` for spear and sword infantry against ranged attacks.
- `defenderAdvantage` — terrain + settlement fortification + winter, from §4 above. The
  attacker's damage is reduced by X% and the defender's raised by X%.

### Range → tiles — **[GEN]**, owner to approve
Unit ranges are given as 30 and 40 but the battlefield is 10 tiles across, so the two scales
must be reconciled. Proposal: **range ÷ 10 = tiles**. Archer 40 → 4 tiles, Skirmisher 30 → 3,
Cavalry Archer 30 → 3.

There is **no morale system** — the 3× unit-count rout rule replaces it.

## 7. Saves

- **IndexedDB**, multiple named slots.
- Autosave on every month rollover, plus manual saves. Rotation depth **[OPEN]**, default 5.
- **JSON export/import** so saves survive a cache wipe and can move to the mobile build.
- Schema-versioned and migrated on load.

Autosave rotation, 8 slots in total:

- **5 monthly** — written at the start of each month, oldest overwritten.
- **3 yearly** — written at the start of a year, covering the last 3 years.

So a campaign always has fine-grained recovery for the last five months and a coarse fallback
stretching three years back.

Determinism is a hard requirement: seeded RNG, integer tick counter, no wall-clock or float
drift in simulation state. The same save plus the same inputs must produce the same outcome
at any speed setting or frame rate.
